## Problem Statement

Circalify is an MIT JavaScript library that renders circular timelines (Danish: aarshjul). After a year with zero promotion the people who show intent are not developers. They are Danish and Nordic non-developers who download the HTML templates to make an organisation's aarshjul. The only tool for them today is Plandisc at EUR 27 to 39 per user per month with no free plan, or a dead PowerPoint template. The founder wants to know whether a hosted, no-code, Danish-first editor at a flat organisation price is something these people will pay for, before building it.

This design covers the test, not the editor.

## Demand Evidence

- About 5,900 npm downloads total, flat at 350 to 400 per month since launch on 18 Oct 2025. This measures the distribution channel, not the market.
- About 70 web3forms submissions, roughly 45 unique people, since 25 Oct 2025. Since the August 2026 form fix, about 1 real signup per week.
- Signups include a Danish government agency, a Danish company, a Norwegian company, a Swedish company, a UK agency, a Finnish analytics company and a Danish national non-profit federation.
- Five outreach rounds to about 45 people produced 2 replies. Both were real usage:
  - A Finnish analytics company answered "A" (used it in something real). No reply to the follow-up.
  - The national chairman of a Danish non-profit federation wrote on 6 Aug 2026 that he uses Circalify to generate the organisation's aarshjul and called it valuable. He uses the HTML templates, not the npm package.
- Nobody has paid or been asked to pay. Willingness to pay is untested.
- Founder's own read, verbatim: "the people with intent are NON-developers who want an aarshjul, not devs who want a library."

## Status Quo

Unknown in detail, and that is the first gap to close. Founder, verbatim: "I know the chairman uses the templates but I haven't asked about time, steps, or what he'd pay." The founder has never watched anyone make a wheel with the templates; he has only seen the output. A 15-minute call with the chairman was requested on 16 Oct 2026 and is awaiting reply.

What is known from Danish sources: the common aarshjul workflow is an Excel template, a PowerPoint sunburst chart, or Plandisc. The chairman chose to hand-edit raw HTML over all three, which the cold read called the strongest behavioural signal in the dataset: the pain is editing and owning the result, not rendering.

## Target User and Narrowest Wedge

Target user: one Danish non-developer who makes a single organisation's aarshjul and needs others to see it. Chairman of a forening, administrator in a kommune team, planner in an agency. One author, many readers.

What the product is: hosting, sharing and updating a living wheel, not rendering one. In 2026 an AI assistant can generate an aarshjul from the MIT library in one prompt, and the chairman may already work that way; the door therefore sells the hosted link that stays current when the plan changes, not the editing screen. If conversations show that nobody needs the link, the editor is not the product either (CEO voice finding 10).

Narrowest wedge chosen (D3): "Rediger dit aarshjul online og del det med et link." Edit in the browser, get a share link, no code, one flat price per organisation, viewers free. Not on the door: Teams/SharePoint embed, per-editor seats, PDF export, templates gallery. Those become interview questions.

Rejected during the session:
- Full editor pitch with embed and per-editor pricing: four features and a subscription on one door; a click would not say which one it was for.
- Done-for-you service at a one-off price: tests a service, not software.
- Paid Danish template pack: static templates are a commodity by the founder's own market read; a yes would not point at the editor.

## Constraints

- Library stays free and MIT. It is the distribution channel.
- No editor is built in this phase. Founder, verbatim: "Do NOT build the editor yet. I'm testing willingness to pay first."
- No backend. Persistence is web3forms (form to inbox, already in use, access key already on the page). Measurement adds one cookieless counter script (GoatCounter); accepted in D9 as the same class of third party as web3forms, not a backend.
- Danish-first copy.
- All site edits land on the gh-pages branch, starting from origin/gh-pages (local gh-pages is one commit behind). No site files on master; the design doc under docs/designs/ and TODOS.md are the only master edits. The founder reviews before any push; a push is a deploy.
- web3forms free plan allows 250 submissions per month; expected volume is far below that.

## Premises

Agreed in D6, D7 and D9.

1. The buyer is a Danish non-developer who makes one org's aarshjul and needs others to see it. npm downloads measure distribution, not market.
2. The door sells one thing at one price: edit online and share by link, flat per organisation, viewers free. No embed on the door.
3. The door lives on a new Danish landing page the posts can link to (revised from "English hero" in D10 after the traffic commitment in D9). The English developer page is unchanged except one Danish link line, the GoatCounter script tag (CEO-0G-3) and the corrected share-card meta hosts (DX-9).
4. web3forms records the two signals: every door click posts a submission with subject "Pro: klik paa early access" (no email), and a submitted email posts a submission with subject "Pro: early access tilmelding". There is no double opt-in, because there is no backend; the founder's personal reply is the only confirmation. GoatCounter records unique visitors and the door-click event so clicks can be read as a rate. GoatCounter is authoritative for visitors and click rate; web3forms is the cross-check for clicks and the source of record for emails.
5. The success bar is set before launch (revised in D9; original bar was unreachable at about 9 template downloads per 60 days). See Success Criteria.
6. After the click the visitor is told the truth: it is not built yet, they are on the early-access list, no payment until they have tried it. Every email gets a personal reply within a few days asking what they do today, and the explicit money question: "Vil I betale 349 kr./md. eller 3.490 kr./aar for det, naar det findes, ja eller nej, og hvad ville goere det til et nej?" The yes/no answers are recorded in the monthly row. Whether a written yes becomes a pass condition is User Challenge UC1 at the final gate; until decided the gate stays as logged.
7. Site edits land only on gh-pages; the design doc and TODOS.md are the only master edits; the founder reviews before pushing.
8. Code is not the moat. Hosted wheels, Danish distribution, price position and trust are. Library stays MIT; editor, hosting, sharing and embed stay closed.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Codex was not installed; a fresh Claude subagent did the cold read from a structured summary. Close paraphrase:

- Steelman: every Danish forening, school, board and kommune team makes one aarshjul a year in PowerPoint or a free template and then cannot update or share it as a living thing. How he distributes the wheel today (PDF, printout, link) decides whether "share by link" is the right verb. "One screen-share call beats a 60-day fake door."
- Wrong premise, rated Critical: the original success bar. At about 9 template downloads per 60 days, "10 early-access emails" needs more paid intent than total free intent, and "30 percent of downloads" passes on 3 clicks. With no visitor denominator the founder cannot tell "nobody saw it" from "nobody wanted it". Fix: a cookieless counter plus 300 or more Danish visitors in the window, or replace the gate with five customer conversations. Accepted; premise 5 revised.
- 48-hour prototype: two hours of screen-share with the chairman first, then a Danish page with a client-side mini-editor whose "Gem og del som link" button is the door, posting the wheel JSON with each signup. Carried as Approach C; deferred until the gate passes.
- Moat: "The founder is right that the code is not a moat and wrong that this matters." Plandisc held the market a decade on brand, Microsoft integration, procurement trust and hosted wheels, none of which exist here yet. "The real risk at zero paying users is not being copied, it is being ignored; nobody clones a product with no revenue."

Where this session disagreed: the mini-editor is the first half of the editor the founder ruled out. It is the free tier if the gate passes, not the test.

The cold read's prototype and the August 2026 "maker" plan. Effort L (human: 2 weeks, CC: 1 to 2 days). Deferred: it is the editor's first half, delays the test by days, and bugs in it would contaminate the signal. Becomes the free tier if the gate passes.

## Success Criteria

Window: 60 days from the day the door goes live, with one bounded extension described under the visitor rule. All four criteria are always read over the same window: day 1 to day 60, or, if the extension is used, day 1 to the day the visitor count reaches 300 or day 90, whichever comes first. Clicks, emails and conversations arriving inside an extension count.

Pass requires all of:
- At least 300 unique visitors to the Danish page (served path /circalify/aarshjul/) in the window. The founder owns driving them.
- Door click rate at or above 5 percent: unique door-click events divided by unique visitors. Basis: the 5 percent figure is the rule of thumb in fake-door vendor guides (Koji, Amplitude, Userpilot; office-hours landscape search 2026-10-16), not a measurement from this site; with 300 visitors it is 15 clicks, so a result within one or two clicks of the bar is reported to the founder as a judgment call, not silently rounded either way.
- At least 5 early-access emails.
- At least 2 completed conversations in the window (call or substantive email exchange) with people reachable through the test: signups, commenters on the posts, or existing inbox contacts who were sent the Danish page. Each records: who makes the wheel, how they do it today, who looks at it, how it is distributed, what they would pay. The chairman call does not count; it happens before launch.

Why 2 conversations and not 3: a door click leaves no contact by design, so conversations can only come from signups and the post threads. The outreach history is 2 replies from about 45 people, so requiring 3 conversations out of a minimum of 5 signups was not credible. 2 out of 5 signups plus the post threads is a stretch and reachable.

How it is counted:
- Visitors = GoatCounter unique visitors for the served path /circalify/aarshjul/. "Unique" means GoatCounter's session-based count: one site + IP + User-Agent combination is mapped to a random ID held in memory for up to eight hours, and the dashboard shows only the first pageview of each session. A visitor returning after eight hours counts again; both sides of the rate use the same figure, so the approximation cancels. The founder's own traffic is excluded three ways: GoatCounter's Settings > Tracking > Ignore IPs for the founder's home and office IPs, the #toggle-goatcounter URL fragment on every browser used for testing, and a localStorage flag (circalify-test) that the founder sets by hand on his own browsers only and that the page honours by skipping the door-click, signup, poll and template-download POSTs while still delivering the zip. Nothing is ever written to a visitor's device; the only reads are the circalify-test check and GoatCounter's own skipgc check, both absent for visitors.
- Clicks = GoatCounter unique count for the event "pro-door-click", deduplicated on the same session basis as visitors, so both sides of the rate are measured the same way. The page stores nothing on the visitor's device to achieve this: the inline panel's open state lives in page memory only, and a reload simply shows the button again. Both the GoatCounter event and the web3forms "Pro: klik paa early access" POST are sent on every click. GoatCounter's dashboard exposes only the unique (first-in-session) count; its non-unique total needs individual-pageview storage enabled, which this plan does not do. The cross-check is therefore: web3forms total clicks must be at least the GoatCounter unique click count (a repeat click within a session raises web3forms but not GoatCounter) and no more than twice it. Outside that band, check ad blockers (which block gc.zgo.at but not web3forms) and bot submissions first; the band is advisory, GoatCounter is authoritative. Diagnostic only, not a pass criterion: clicks divided by unique "pro-door-seen" events tells whether a low rate is visibility or demand; with the door in the hero the two denominators should be close.
- Emails = unique addresses, excluding the founder's own, excluding obvious spam, counted after the personal reply has been sent. A bounce removes the address from the count.
- Free-intent baseline = template downloads posted from /aarshjul/ (distinct subject line and page field), read monthly next to door clicks and signups.

Visitor rule: if there are fewer than 300 unique visitors after 60 days, the test is invalid, not failed. One extension of at most 30 days is allowed, with a second posting round, and the other three criteria are read over the same extended window so the rate is never measured on mismatched periods. If the count is still under 300 on day 90, the fake door comes down and the question moves to five customer conversations (the cold read's alternative) instead of a door. No further extensions.

Pass means: the August "validation gate" is passed. Build the maker (Approach C) as the free tier, then hosted save and share as Pro. Fail means: the door comes down, the Danish page stays as the Danish entry point (a single page on github.io will not rank for aarshjul by itself; sitemap and robots.txt are hygiene, not a ranking plan), and the founder writes up what the conversations said before deciding anything else.

#### Section 4: Data Flow & Interaction Edge Cases

```
  SIGNUP FLOW
  INPUT(email, org) -> VALIDATE(checkValidity, maxlength) -> TRANSFORM(FormData + subject + source) -> PERSIST(POST web3forms, awaited) -> OUTPUT(success line | error line)

     shadow: timeout/network -> error line, value kept   shadow: 429 quota -> error line
     shadow: duplicate submit -> button disabled during await
     shadow: stale state (user edits after success) -> panel stays in success; reload to resubmit

  DOOR CLICK / POLL / DOWNLOAD (fire-and-forget)
  INPUT(click) -> TRANSFORM(FormData) -> PERSIST(POST keepalive) + gcEvent -> OUTPUT(panel | thanks | zip)
     shadow: navigate away -> keepalive lets the POST finish
     shadow: GC blocked -> guard skips; web3forms still posts
```

Async ordering: the only shared mutable state is the door panel's in-memory state. Invariant: one signup POST per submit. Regression proof: static assertion that the handler disables the button (check-pages.mjs) plus the deployed-page manual check; a unit harness is out of scope for a static page.
